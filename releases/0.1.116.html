Bello Agent 0.1.116

Changed

  • Thousands read “K” everywhere. The Usage Report wrote “12.3k” where the rest of the app wrote “12.3K”. Six other places wrote “k” too:
    • the sidebar's counts;
    • the context meter;
    • the menu bar;
    • tokens per second;
    • the transcript's compact counts;
    • the model catalog's context sizes.
    Each keeps its own decimals.
  • Money reads with two decimals everywhere: “$1.50”, never “$1.5”; “$0.00”, never “$0”. Tiny amounts keep the digits they need. The cost charts in the menu bar and the Usage Report now label their axes in dollars.
  • The app's own controls throughout. The remaining stock macOS controls now use the app's look, at the same sizes, with the same keyboard access:
    • spinners;
    • the loading bar of a captured response;
    • switches;
    • checkboxes;
    • the Usage Report's date fields.
  • The large-table window has the app's look. It has the app's window bar and Copy button, a rounded grid with a sunken header and a highlighted selected row, and the app's detail area.
  • The footer under the composer stays still while you read.
    • The capture badge keeps the room it had when the chat was shown, so “Next: ” coming and going when the helper opens or leaves no longer moves the conversation.
    • A notice (“Saved history · Host runtime unloaded”) takes only the room the footer has free, and is shortened there, with its whole text in the tooltip.
    • During a run, a notice now sits left of the clock.
  • A side whose chat was deleted opens no side of its own. It stays in the sidebar as a chat. Open Side and /side say why: “The chat this side was opened from was deleted, so it can't open sides of its own.”

Fixed

  • The sidebar's chevron folds the sides of any chat. You could fold or unfold a chat's sides only while that chat was open; for any other chat the chevron did nothing (since 0.1.100).
  • Sheets no longer update inside SwiftUI's own update. When the window behind an open sheet changed its reduce-motion or enabled state, the sheet was told inside SwiftUI's update, which SwiftUI reports as undefined behaviour. It is now told just after.
  • The helper encodes each reply once. It encoded every reply twice: once to check its size and once to write it. Reading 4,000 snapshot and history pages (32 MB of replies) now takes 32% less of the helper's CPU (median 8.95 s → 6.13 s over five runs of a Release build). What the app receives is byte for byte the same.
